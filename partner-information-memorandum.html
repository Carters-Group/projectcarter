Introduction

I'm Trent Macartney, a developer and value-add commercial investor. I go after the sites and buildings other people walk away from, and do the work to turn them into something worth keeping.

Most of my work is on my own account: heritage-inspired homes in Toowoomba, a commercial freehold being repositioned in Melbourne, and boutique residential projects taken from bare site to sold homes. This memorandum explains how I now offer the same process to investors who have the capital or the land, but not the time or the team to run a project themselves.

Why this exists

Investors with money or a site usually end up with one of three options, and none of them is a great fit:

  • A buyer's agent finds and buys the asset, then the job ends at settlement. The approvals, delivery and exit, which is where the value is made, are left with you.
  • A passive fund takes your capital, but you don't own the asset, you don't control the decisions, and fees run every year whether the result comes or not.
  • Doing it yourself means managing consultants, councils, builders and agents for two or three years, usually on top of a business or career.

The partnership sits in between. You own the asset outright. I take on the responsibility for delivering it, and most of my reward comes from the uplift.

How it works

Every project runs through four phases. Each one ends with a decision point where you see the numbers and approve the next step before money is committed.

Phase 1

Strategy & due diligence

  • Investment brief: goals, capital, risk appetite, timeframe and target return
  • Site search, on and off market, against the brief
  • Planning, title, physical, environmental and market due diligence
  • Feasibility, business plan, budget and program

Decision point: you approve the site, the business plan and the budget.

Phase 2

Acquisition

  • Price and contract negotiation, including conditions that protect the plan
  • Finance introductions, lender packs and valuation
  • Coordination with your solicitor and accountant on structure and settlement

Decision point: you sign the contract and settle, in your own name or entity.

Phase 3

Approvals & value-add

  • Design, consultant team and council approvals for development
  • Re-leasing, refurbishment, signage and new income for commercial
  • Builder tender, pricing comparisons and contract recommendation
  • Monthly reporting on budget, program and risk

Decision point: you approve the builder or works contract and any material budget change.

Phase 4

Delivery & exit

  • Project management through construction or repositioning
  • Final approvals, defects and handover
  • Sales strategy: off the plan, on completion, or as a whole asset
  • Or independent revaluation and refinance if you're holding

Decision point: you approve the sale or the refinance.

Reserved matters: the decisions that stay with you

  • Buying the site or asset, and the contract price
  • The business plan, budget and target return
  • Spending beyond the approved budget tolerance
  • Appointing the builder or the main works contractor
  • New borrowing, refinancing or giving security over the asset
  • Selling the asset, or the sales strategy for an off the plan release

Logic over feel. Every recommendation comes with the feasibility and the options behind it. Reserved matters have a set turnaround time in the agreement, so a project is never left waiting on a decision.

Reporting

A monthly report covering spend against budget, the program, approvals status, risks and the current feasibility. At each decision point, a short decision paper with the recommendation and the numbers.

How uplift is measured

  • Development: net profit on sale after every project cost: land, acquisition costs, finance, consultants, authority fees, construction, selling costs and the engagement and acquisition fees.
  • Value-add commercial: an independent valuation at completion, less the total cost base: purchase, acquisition costs, capital works, fees and holding costs net of rental income.
  • Payment timing: on sale for a development. For an asset you keep, on revaluation at completion, with the option to defer to a later sale or refinance.
  • Third-party costs: consultants, builders, authority fees and agents are project costs paid by the owner, and are included before uplift is calculated.

  • Wants to make the big calls with the numbers in front of you, not manage trades
