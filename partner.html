How it works

Four phases over a typical two to three year term, depending on the project. Each one ends with a clear decision point, so you always know where it stands.

01

Strategy & due diligence

Your goals, capital and timeframe set the brief. Then the search and the hard look at each site.

  • Investment brief and target return
  • Site search, on and off market
  • Planning, title, physical and market due diligence
  • Feasibility and business plan for your sign-off
02

Acquisition

Buying it right, on terms that protect the plan.

  • Negotiation and contract terms
  • Finance introductions and lender packs
  • Coordinating legal, valuation and settlement
  • Ownership structure set up with your advisers
03

Approvals & value-add

Where most of the uplift is made: getting to the approval or income target.

  • Design, consultants and council approvals
  • Re-leasing, refurbishment and new income on commercial
  • Builder tender and pricing comparisons
  • Budget, program and monthly reporting
04

Delivery & exit

Building or repositioning to completion, then realising the value.

  • Project management through construction
  • Final approvals and handover
  • Sales strategy, off the plan or on completion
  • Or revaluation and refinance if you're holding

Clear roles, fast decisions

Keeping ownership and delivery separate is what keeps a project moving. You hold the asset. I hold the responsibility for delivering it.

You, the owner

  • Own the asset in your own name, company or trust
  • Provide the capital or the land, and the finance with my help
  • Approve the business plan and budget up front
  • Sign off the big calls: purchase, builder, budget changes, sale or refinance

Me, the delivery partner

  • Run the project day to day against the approved plan
  • Find, brief and manage the consultants, builders and agents
  • Bring every big decision to you with a recommendation and the numbers behind it
  • Report monthly on budget, program and risks

Decisions are made on the numbers, not on feel. Every recommendation comes with the feasibility behind it, and big decisions have a set turnaround time, so the project never sits waiting.

What "uplift" means

Agreed in writing before we start, so there's never a debate at the end.

  • Development: the net profit on sale after every project cost, including land, finance, consultants, build and my engagement and acquisition fees.
  • Value-add commercial: an independent valuation at completion, less your total cost base: purchase, acquisition costs, capital works and holding costs net of rent.
  • No uplift: you've paid the engagement and acquisition fees only. There's no uplift share to pay.
  • Third parties: consultants, builders, authority fees and agents are project costs paid by the owner, as on any project. What you don't pay is an ongoing fee for my time.

Who it's for

This works best for investors who want a real asset and a strong result, without running the project themselves.

  • Cash or equity of $500K or more, or a site you already own
  • Deals from around $1.5M purchase price or land value
  • A two to three year horizon
  • Happy to own the asset in your own name or entity
  • Open to development, value-add commercial, or either
  • Wants to be kept informed and make the big calls, not manage trades
  • Sophisticated or wholesale investors preferred

Questions investors ask

What happens if there's no uplift?

Then there's no uplift share. You'll have paid the engagement fee and the acquisition fee, and nothing more to me. That's deliberate: most of my reward depends on the result I deliver for you.

Do I need development experience?

No. That's the point of the partnership. You make the big decisions with the numbers in front of you, and I handle the rest.

I already own a site. How does it work?

The same way, minus the purchase. The 2.5% is calculated on an agreed land value at the start, less the $20,000 engagement fee, and the uplift is measured from that value.

Who makes the decisions?

You approve the business plan and budget at the start, and sign off the big calls: the purchase, the builder, any material budget change, and the sale or refinance. I run everything else day to day against the approved plan, and report to you monthly.

When is the uplift share paid?

At the exit point we agree up front: on sale for a development, or on an independent revaluation at completion for an asset you're keeping. If you're holding, it can be deferred to a later sale or refinance so you're not forced to sell to pay it.

Who pays the consultants and the builder?

The owner, as on any project. Consultants, builders, authority fees and agents are project costs and come out before the uplift is calculated. What you don't pay is an ongoing management fee for my time.

How long does a project take?

Usually two to three years from engagement to exit, depending on the approvals involved and whether it's a development or a commercial repositioning.

Is this an investment product?

No. You buy and own the property directly, and engage me to manage the project under a written agreement. I don't hold your money or pool it with other investors. You should get your own legal, tax and financial advice before you commit.
