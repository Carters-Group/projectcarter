How the numbers work

  • This is a deposit / equity guide, not a borrowing capacity or credit assessment. It shows the minimum deposit or equity a lender in each tier typically expects for the loan type you pick, given the price or total development cost you enter. What you can actually borrow depends on income, serviceability, credit history and the specific security — that assessment happens with a registered brokerage.
  • Major banks are the big four and other large deposit-taking banks. They generally offer the sharpest rates for the lowest-risk, most straightforward deals, and are the most conservative on leverage and documentation.
  • Tier 2 / non-bank lenders covers second-tier banks, credit unions, private and specialist commercial lenders. They typically lend at a higher loan-to-value ratio (a smaller deposit) and are more flexible on documentation and credit, for a higher interest rate.
  • Owner-occupied commercial is a business buying or refinancing the premises it trades from. This is the lowest-risk category for lenders and generally attracts the highest leverage and sharpest rates.
  • Commercial investment is a property leased to a tenant, assessed mainly on the strength of the lease and tenant covenant rather than the owner's own trade.
  • SMSF commercial property is bought through a limited recourse borrowing arrangement (LRBA) inside a self-managed super fund, including a business buying its own premises back into its members' SMSF (a "rent-back"). Most major banks have stepped back from this market, so it is largely written by specialist SMSF / LRBA lenders.
  • Lease doc / low doc investment is serviced mainly by the property's own lease rather than the borrower's financials. Lower deposit requirements from the majors generally aren't available; this is tier 2 / non-bank territory.
  • Build to Rent (BTR) uses the total development cost as the base for lending rather than a purchase price. Lenders only recognise part of the projected rental income toward covering debt costs — the rental cover check below uses a 75% recognition of gross rent as a rough guide.
  • Build to sell (development / construction for sale) also lends against total development cost. Major banks typically want a level of qualifying presales in place before funding; tier 2 / non-bank lenders are more likely to fund with less presale cover, priced according to the deposit or equity contributed.
  • All loan-to-value ratios, deposit percentages and rate ranges shown are indicative starting points based on typical market settings as at the time of writing. Every lender, deal and security is different — treat these as a planning guide, not a quote or approval.
