# Clive Palmer and Queensland Nickel: sub-30 second reels

Format: one fact, one twist, one closing line. About 60 to 75 words each (roughly 25 to 28 seconds spoken).
Items marked (verify) still need checking against the original article before posting.

**Legal care for every reel:** Palmer sues often. Stick to what courts found, what liquidators and administrators claimed (say "claimed" or "alleged"), and what he said in reply. Palmer has never been convicted over Queensland Nickel. Never say he stole, or call it a scam. Ask the questions and let viewers decide.

## Reel 1: 800 jobs gone overnight (2016) [strongest]
**Hook (on screen):** "His refinery went broke. You paid the workers."
> January 2016. Clive Palmer's Queensland Nickel refinery near Townsville goes into administration. 237 workers are sacked straight away. Within weeks it's about 800 jobs gone, and debts of at least $226 million. The workers' unpaid entitlements? Taxpayers covered them through a government scheme, about $66 million. Palmer said the payouts were not his burden. Three years later, he settled and agreed to pay it back.
**Close:** "Should taxpayers ever foot that bill?"
Accuracy notes:
- The taxpayer figure is reported as $66m to just over $70m. Use "about $66 million".
- The 2019 settlement (about $110m, August 2019) covered full repayment of the government's Fair Entitlements Guarantee money and other worker entitlements, according to the liquidator, Stephen Parbery.
- Palmer had asked the Queensland government for a $35m loan in late 2015 and was refused. Mention it if you want balance.

## Reel 2: Who is Terry Smith? [strong hook]
**Hook:** "The boss signed his emails Terry Smith."
> 2016. Administrators dig through Queensland Nickel's records. They find spending approved by someone called "Terry Smith", including an $8 million haulage contract and a $6.3 million mining contract. Terry Smith was Clive Palmer. He used the alias after he entered parliament in 2013. The administrators said Palmer appeared to act as a shadow director. Palmer said nothing was suspicious: staff knew it was him.
**Close:** "Why would you need a fake name?"
Accuracy notes:
- "Shadow director" was the administrators' view (FTI Consulting, April 2016), not a court finding. Say so.
- Palmer's nephew, Clive Mensink, was the official director.
Sources: https://www.miningweekly.com/article/report-claims-queensland-nickel-was-used-to-fund-other-palmer-interests-2016-04-13 · https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/news/2016/04/11/palmer-queensland-nickel · https://indaily.com.au/news/2016/04/12/palmer-denies-secret-hand-in-troubled-business

## Reel 3: The nephew who never came back [very timely: October 2026]
**Hook:** "He left Australia. Ten years later, he's guilty."
> Clive Mensink, Clive Palmer's nephew, was the director of Queensland Nickel when it collapsed. Liquidators wanted to question him under oath. He left the country and never came back to answer. Arrest warrants were issued. This month, October 2026, the Federal Court found him guilty of contempt, tried in his absence. The judge called his excuses for not returning "self-serving".
**Close:** "Where's Clive Mensink? Comment if you know."
Accuracy notes:
- Ruling: Justice Darryl Rangiah, Federal Court, Brisbane, 8 October 2026. Guilty on failing to comply with a summons; a second charge was dismissed. Penalty not yet decided.
- "Ten years later" works because the collapse was 2016. Check the exact date he left (reported as 2016) before putting it on screen. (verify)
- Don't name where he is. Reports differ and it's not needed.

## Reel 4: Where did the money go? (2016 → 2021)
**Hook:** "A nickel refinery. Funding a political party?"
> When Queensland Nickel collapsed, administrators claimed it had paid about $21.5 million in donations to the Palmer United Party, Clive Palmer's own party. They also claimed about $224 million had gone to other Palmer businesses. Palmer called their report a "fantasy". But in 2021, Queensland's Court of Appeal ordered his main company, Mineralogy, to pay back about $102 million in loans from the refinery.
**Close:** "Follow the money."
Accuracy notes:
- The donations and $224m figures are claims from the administrators' 2016 report, not court findings. Some outlets report $26m in donations. Use "about $21.5 million" and "claimed".
- The 2021 Court of Appeal ruling reversed a 2020 Supreme Court win for Palmer. It found the payments were loans from Queensland Nickel to Mineralogy.
Sources: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2021-06-25/qld-palmer-company-mineralogy-to-pay-102m-for-qld-nickel-fail/100245082 · https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-06-03/clive-palmer-and-queensland-nickel-court-ruling/12314878 · https://www.miningweekly.com/article/report-claims-queensland-nickel-was-used-to-fund-other-palmer-interests-2016-04-13

**Hook:** "Shut for six years. Then sold for $2 billion?"
> The Townsville refinery has sat idle since 2016. In 2018, Palmer promised to reopen it and create hundreds of jobs. It didn't happen. In December 2022, he announced a sale to an obscure Swiss company, Zero Carbon Investek, reported at up to $2 billion, with plans for a huge solar farm. Since then? Reports say the buyer hasn't shown it raised the money.
