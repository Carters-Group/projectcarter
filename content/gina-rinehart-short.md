# Gina Rinehart: "The Cost of a Fortune" (~90 sec short)

Angle: three generations, billions of dollars, a family torn apart. What's a fortune worth if your family falls apart?

## Script (~230 words)

**[0:00 – Hook]**
$39 billion. The richest person in Australia. And she's spent more than 30 years in court with her own family.

**[0:08 – Generation one]**
Gina Rinehart's father, Lang Hancock, found a mountain of iron ore in the Pilbara and became one of the richest men in the country. But when he married his housekeeper, Rose, Gina and her father fell out. Near the end, Lang reportedly wrote to her: "Please leave me in peace."

**[0:25 – The fight over his legacy]**
He died in 1992. What followed was 14 years of lawsuits between Gina and her stepmother over his estate.

**[0:35 – Generation two]**
Gina turned her father's company into an empire. But history repeated itself. Lang had left a trust for his grandchildren. In 2011, just days before it was due to pass to them, Gina moved to push it out to 2068. Three of her four children sued their own mother. A judge removed her as trustee.

**[0:55 – The mega-trial]**
Then came a fight over the mines themselves. In 2026, a 1,655-page judgment ruled the mines belong to Gina's company. She won. Her children lost.

**[1:08 – The turn]**
But did anyone really win? Her son said he just wanted the family back together.

**[1:15 – Close]**
Three generations. Billions of dollars. Decades in court. And the one thing all that money couldn't buy was a family that stayed together.
*So what's a fortune worth if there's no one left to share it with?*

## Production notes
- Visual contrast: wealth (mines, jets, Rich List headlines) against emptiness (empty courtrooms, empty dinner table, old family photos).
- Music: grand at the start, stripped back to a single piano from the 1:08 turn.
- End text on screen: "Three generations. Billions of dollars. One family torn apart."
- Legal care: present the fraud claims as allegations only. The court rejected the children's ownership claims and never found fraud. Rinehart is known for suing.
- Before publishing, check the status of Hancock Prospecting's May 2026 appeal.
