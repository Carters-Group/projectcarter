# Malcolm Turnbull: sub-30 second reels

Format: one fact, one twist, one closing line. About 60 to 75 words each (roughly 25 to 28 seconds spoken).
Items marked (verify) come from general knowledge and still need a source before posting.

---

## Reel 1: The fake email that sank him (Utegate, 2009) [strongest]
**Hook (on screen):** "One fake email ended his career."
> 2009. Opposition Leader Malcolm Turnbull accuses Prime Minister Kevin Rudd of doing favours for a car dealer mate. His proof? An email from a Treasury official, Godwin Grech. Turnbull calls on Rudd to resign. Then police investigate, and the email turns out to be fake. Grech had written it himself. Months later, Turnbull lost the Liberal leadership.
**Close:** "Check your sources."
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utegate · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godwin_Grech

## Reel 2: "So what, I'm rich" (Cayman Islands, 2015)
**Hook:** "The PM with money in the Cayman Islands."
> October 2015. Weeks after becoming Prime Minister, Malcolm Turnbull is grilled about investments in the Cayman Islands, a famous tax haven. His fortune is estimated at around $200 million. Was it illegal? No. It was declared, and he says every cent was taxed in Australia. But Labor's message was simple: one set of rules for him, another for you.
**Close:** "Legal. But a good look?"
Sources: https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/so-what-im-rich-says-turnbull/2fkegeqsz · https://theconversation.com/turnbull-says-he-pays-his-full-tax-as-labor-pursues-his-investments-49148

## Reel 3: Live by the knife (2015 → 2018)
**Hook:** "He knifed a Prime Minister. Then it happened to him."
> 2015. Turnbull rolls Tony Abbott and takes the top job (verify: party room vote 54–44). Three years later, Peter Dutton comes for him. August 2018: Turnbull is gone. But Dutton doesn't win either. Scott Morrison slips through the middle, 45 votes to 40. Then Turnbull quits parliament, and his safe Liberal seat of Wentworth swings almost 20% to an independent.
**Close:** "In Canberra, nobody's safe."
Sources: https://www.sbs.com.au/news/liberals-vote-to-dump-turnbull-in-leadership-spill-bishop-out-in-first-round-vote · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Wentworth_by-election

## Reel 4: He beat the British government (Spycatcher, 1986) (verify everything)
**Hook:** "At 31, he took on the British government and won."
> Before politics, Turnbull was a lawyer. In the 1980s, Britain tried to stop ex-MI5 spy Peter Wright publishing his memoir, Spycatcher, in Australia. Turnbull defended him and famously grilled a top British civil servant, who admitted he'd been "economical with the truth". Britain lost. The book became a bestseller.
**Close:** "The case that made Malcolm Turnbull."

## Reel 5: The "has-been" fight (2026) [timely]
**Hook:** "His own side wants him to stop talking."
> 2026. Malcolm Turnbull calls AUKUS, Australia's nuclear submarine deal, "a huge wealth transfer" from Australia to the US and UK. Liberal senator Jacinta Nampijinpa Price tells him to move on, calling him a has-been. Seven years out of office, the former PM is still the Liberals' loudest critic.
**Close:** "Should ex-PMs stay quiet? Comment below."

## Reel 6: The $444 million reef grant (2018) [strong]
**Hook:** "$444 million. No tender. Six staff."
> 2018. The Turnbull government hands $444 million to save the Great Barrier Reef. Not to a government agency. To a small private charity, the Great Barrier Reef Foundation. It had about six full-time staff. It didn't apply for the money, and there was no tender. Months later, the Auditor-General found there were no clear criteria to show it was value for money.
**Close:** "Who decides where your tax dollars go?"
Optional (verify): the grant was reportedly offered at a meeting between Turnbull, Josh Frydenberg and the foundation's chair, and the foundation said it was surprised by the offer.
Legal care: don't suggest anyone personally benefited. The criticism is about the process.

**Follow-up reel: "So what happened to the $444 million?"**
> The money was meant to be spent over six years, to June 2024, on water quality, killing crown-of-thorns starfish, reef restoration science, Indigenous and community programs, and monitoring. Up to $44.3m could go on administration. But the Auditor-General estimated the total admin and fundraising cost could reach about $86 million. By 2022, almost all of it was committed, with only $5.2m left. The foundation says it funded more than 460 projects. Has the reef improved? There's no independent final verdict yet.
Close: "Money well spent? You decide."
Check before posting: whether a final evaluation of the partnership has come out since 2024.

## Reel 7: The $96.5 million weather website (2025) [very timely, partly a Turnbull story]
**Hook:** "They said $4 million. It cost $96.5 million."
> October 2025. The Bureau of Meteorology launches a new website. Australians hate it. The radar is hard to read and the place names are hard to see. 400,000 complaints pour in. Then the new BOM boss reveals the cost: not the $4.1 million first quoted, but $96.5 million, about 20 times more. Most of it went to one contract with Accenture, which grew from $31 million to $78 million over nine extensions. And who approved the overhaul? The Turnbull government, after a 2015 cyber attack.
**Close:** "Was it worth $96 million? Check the radar."
Accuracy notes:
- The figure is $96.5m, not $85m.
- The BOM says the cost covers back-end systems, not just the site you see. The website is about 10% of the wider IT overhaul (ROBUST, reported at close to $800m).
- The Turnbull government approved and funded the program, but the website was built and launched under Labor (Minister Murray Watt). Say that, or the video looks one-sided.
- The executive who ran it, Peter Stone, was reported to be leaving at the end of June 2026.
