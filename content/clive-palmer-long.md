# Clive Palmer: Australians aren't that dumb

**Angle:** Clive Palmer has spent more money chasing Australian votes than almost anyone in our history, about a quarter of a billion dollars. Australians took the texts, read the billboards, and still said no. Then the twist: maybe winning was never the point.
**Length:** about 3 minutes (roughly 480 words spoken).
**Tone:** dry, amused, on the viewer's side. The joke is on Clive, and the viewer gets the credit.

---

## SCRIPT

**[0:00] COLD OPEN**
*On screen: a yellow UAP billboard. Text slams in: "$260,000,000"*

Clive Palmer has spent about two hundred and sixty million dollars trying to get you to vote for him.

*Beat. Text: "Lower house seats since 2013: 0"*

And since 2013, he's won zero lower house seats.

**[0:12] THE SPEND**
*B-roll: billboards, TV ads, phones buzzing.*

2019: eighty-three million dollars. At the time, a record. Every billboard, every ad break, every bus shelter was yellow. Seats won? None.

2022: a hundred and twenty-three million. More than the Labor Party spent. More than any party except the Coalition. Seats won? One, in the Senate.

2025: new name, Trumpet of Patriots. About fifty-three million dollars, plus all those text messages you never signed up for. Seats won? None. One point eight five per cent of the vote.

**[0:45] THE MATHS**
*On screen: a calculator. "$123.5m ÷ ~600,000 votes"*

Break 2022 down and he paid roughly two hundred dollars for every vote he got. He'd have been better off buying everyone a carton of beer and asking nicely.

**[1:00] WHY WE DIDN'T BUY IT**
*B-roll: the rusting Yabulu refinery outside Townsville.*

Maybe it's because people remember. In 2016 his nickel refinery in Townsville collapsed. About 800 workers lost their jobs. Their entitlements? Taxpayers covered them, about sixty-six million dollars, and Palmer said the payouts weren't his burden. He did pay it back, three years and a lot of court later.

*On screen: an email signed "Terry Smith"*

The administrators said he'd been approving company spending under another name, "Terry Smith". Palmer says there was nothing suspicious about it, because staff knew it was him. And the company's director, his nephew, left the country instead of answering the liquidators' questions. This month, a court found the nephew guilty of contempt.

So when that same man spends a hundred million dollars telling you he'll fix the country... Australians aren't that dumb.

**[1:45] THE TWIST**
*Music drops out.*

But here's the thing. Maybe he wasn't trying to win.

After the 2019 election, Palmer said his real goal was stopping Bill Shorten. His ads hammered Labor, and most of his preferences flowed to the Coalition. Labor's own review said his spending swamped their advertising. Palmer was claiming credit within days.

*On screen: "Didn't win a seat. Claims he picked the government."*

He couldn't buy your vote. So he bought the conversation instead.

**[2:15] THE ENDING**
*B-roll: Parliament House.*

Parliament has now passed laws capping how much anyone can donate and spend at elections. Guess who's taking them to the High Court?

*On screen: Clive Palmer, smiling.*

The man who spent a quarter of a billion dollars on politics and got almost nothing for it says the caps attack his political freedom.

**[2:35] CLOSE**
*Text on black: "$260,000,000. 0 lower house seats."*

Two hundred and sixty million dollars. One lesson. You can buy every billboard in the country.

*Beat.*

You can't buy Australians.

*Caption / end card:* "Should billionaires be allowed to spend unlimited money on elections? 👇"

## Check before posting
- **$260m total:** $83.6m (2019) + $123.5m (2022) + about $53m (2025, AEC figure; Palmer says $60m). Some outlets estimate 2022 lower ($70m to $100m). On screen, "about $260m" or "a quarter of a billion" is safe.
- **"Zero lower house seats since 2013":** Palmer won Fairfax himself in 2013. Since then, no lower house seats. The 2022 Senate seat was Ralph Babet, who later left the party. (verify Babet's current party)
- **"More than Labor spent" (2022):** from The Conversation's analysis. The Coalition spent more.
- **"$200 a vote":** worked out from $123.5m and the UAP's 2022 House vote of about 4.1%, roughly 600,000 votes. Check the AEC vote total before putting the number on screen. In 2022 the party got about $1.9m back in public funding.
- **The 2019 twist:** "his preferences decided it" is Palmer's own claim. The Labor review (Emerson and Weatherill) said his spending "disrupted any impact" Labor's own advertising could have had. The script says exactly that, no more.
- **Spending caps:** the reforms cap donations at $50,000 and spending at $800,000 per seat. Check the start date (reported as mid-2026, possibly delayed) and whether his High Court challenge has been filed or heard.
- **Mensink:** found guilty of contempt on 8 October 2026. His penalty hasn't been set.
- **Check the quote:** the line "attack his political freedom" paraphrases reports that he says the caps infringe political freedoms. Find his exact words and quote them if you can.
- **Legal care:** no "scam", "stole" or "fraud" about Palmer. He's never been convicted over Queensland Nickel. Keep "Administrators found" and "Palmer said" as written.
