# Clive Palmer: the long one

**Working title:** "Clive Palmer: $250 million, 800 sacked workers, zero seats"
**Length:** about 4 minutes spoken (roughly 620 words).
**Tone:** sarcastic, but every line is a fact, a court finding, or a claim labelled as a claim. The facts make him look bad on their own, so the script never has to call him anything.

**Legal care:** Palmer sues often, and he has the money to do it. On screen and in the caption:
- Never say he stole, scammed or committed fraud. He has never been convicted over Queensland Nickel. The ASIC fraud charges (Part 5) are still unproven.
- Keep "claimed", "alleged" and "the court found" exactly where they are in the script.
- No swearing about him in the title, thumbnail or caption. Put it in the comments if you have to.

---

## Cold open (0:00 to 0:15)
**On screen:** "$250,000,000"
> Clive Palmer has spent more than $250 million trying to win elections. He's won one lower house seat in twelve years. But before you laugh, remember who paid for his refinery's workers when it went broke. You did.

## Part 1: The refinery (0:15 to 0:50)
> 2009. Palmer buys a nickel refinery at Yabulu, just outside Townsville, from BHP. It's one of the town's biggest employers. January 2016. Nickel prices crash, and Queensland Nickel goes under. 237 workers are sacked straight away. Within weeks, about 800 jobs are gone, with debts of at least $226 million.
> The workers are owed their entitlements: leave, redundancy, notice. Who pays? Not Clive. Taxpayers do, through a government safety-net scheme, about $66 million of your money. Palmer's line: the payouts were not his burden.

**Visual:** aerial shot of the rusting refinery, then a "$66,000,000" counter ticking up.

## Part 2: Who is Terry Smith? (0:50 to 1:20)
> Then the administrators go through the books. They find millions in spending signed off by a man called Terry Smith: an $8 million haulage contract, a $6.3 million mining contract. There is no Terry Smith. It's Clive Palmer, using a fake name on his emails after he entered parliament. The administrators said he appeared to be running the company as a shadow director. Palmer said there was nothing suspicious, because the staff knew it was him.

**On screen:** an email signed "Terry Smith", then the name crossed out and "Clive" written over it.

## Part 3: Follow the money (1:20 to 1:55)
> So where did the refinery's money go? The administrators claimed about $21.5 million went in donations to the Palmer United Party, Clive's own party. They claimed about $224 million had gone to other Palmer businesses. Palmer called their report a "fantasy". But in 2021, Queensland's Court of Appeal ordered his company Mineralogy to pay back about $102 million it had borrowed from the refinery. In 2019 he'd already settled the rest, after three years of court fights, including taxpayers' $66 million.

## Part 4: The nephew (1:55 to 2:20)
> The official director was Palmer's nephew, Clive Mensink. When liquidators wanted to question him under oath, he left Australia and never came back. Arrest warrants were issued. Ten years later, this month, the Federal Court found him guilty of contempt, in a trial held without him there. The judge called his excuses "self-serving".

## Part 5: Buying democracy, badly (2:20 to 2:55)
> Meanwhile, Clive was busy with politics. In 2019 he spent $83.6 million on ads. Seats won: zero. In 2022, $123.5 million. One Senate seat. In 2025, with a new party called Trumpet of Patriots, about $53 million plus all those text messages you didn't ask for. Seats won: zero.
> And one more thing. ASIC has charged him with fraud and dishonest use of his position as a director over more than $12 million moved from Mineralogy to his party before the 2013 election. He denies it, says it's political, and has spent years trying to get the case thrown out. He has not been convicted.

**Visual:** stack of money burning, then a wall of "UAP" billboards, then a phone buzzing with spam texts.

## Part 6: The sore loser (2:55 to 3:35)
> When he doesn't get his way, he sues. Western Australia blocked his $30 billion damages claim over an iron ore project. He took it to the High Court and lost. He sued over WA's COVID border closure and lost. Then he moved his Australian assets to a company in Singapore, and used a trade deal to sue Australia, the country, for nearly $300 billion. In September 2025, the tribunal threw it out and ordered him to pay Australia $13.6 million in costs.
> Oh, and his 2019 election song? He ripped off Twisted Sister's "We're Not Gonna Take It". The Federal Court made him pay $1.5 million and called it "opportunistic".

**Audio:** a few seconds of "Aussies Not Gonna Cop It" cutting off with a record scratch. Check the rights first, or play a soundalike.

## Close (3:35 to 4:00)
> So, the scorecard. 800 workers sacked. $66 million of your money to pay them, eventually paid back. More than $250 million on elections, and one lower house seat in twelve years. A nephew convicted of contempt. A $300 billion lawsuit against his own country, thrown out. And Clive Palmer is still a billionaire.

**Close (on screen):** "Who's not gonna cop it now?"

## Optional extras (verify before using)
- **Hydroxychloroquine, 2020:** Palmer bought about 32.9 million doses to fight COVID, and the Morrison government accepted them into the national stockpile. Tests showed the drug didn't work against COVID, and about 5 million doses were reportedly sent for destruction. Good for a 15-second cutaway.
- **Titanic II:** announced in 2012, re-announced in 2018 and again in 2024. It still hasn't been built. (verify the 2024 date)
- **"Chinese mongrels", 2014:** his comments on ABC's Q&A while he was a federal MP. (verify the exact quote before using)
- **The lawyer, October 2026:** a lawyer who acted for Palmer, Sam Iskander, is accused of misappropriating about $34 million from Mineralogy, and police sought an arrest warrant. This is alleged against the lawyer, not Palmer.
- **Retirement:** reports say Palmer announced he's retiring from politics after 2025. (verify before saying it)

## Fact-check list before posting
- One lower house seat in twelve years: Palmer himself won Fairfax in 2013. Since then, no lower house seats. If you'd rather avoid the exact count, say "barely anything".
- The $250m+ total: $83.6m (2019) + $123.5m (2022) + about $53m (2025, AEC figure; Palmer claims $60m) comes to about $260m, before counting 2013.
- The taxpayer figure is reported as $66m to just over $70m.
- The $21.5m and $224m figures are claims from the administrators' 2016 report, not court findings.
- Check the date Mensink left Australia (reported as 2016). His penalty hasn't been set yet.
- The ASIC case: confirm it's still unresolved before posting. If there's a verdict, Part 5 changes.
